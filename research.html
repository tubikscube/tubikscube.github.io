Research & publications

01 / Research statement

My research examines the relationships between disaster recovery, community resilience and place attachment — asking how people rebuild a sense of place after environmental disaster. Using qualitative and participatory methods, I study how environmental disruption is lived through memory, emotion and everyday experience, and how recovery practice can respond to loss beyond the physical landscape.

Research interests Place attachment, Memoryscapes, Disaster recovery, Community resilience, Climate adaptation, Qualitative methods, Walking audio, Photo elicitation, Interpretive phenomenology

Disaster & recovery

Recovery as a social, emotional and cultural process — not only physical reconstruction.

Place, memory & loss

How people’s bonds to landscape persist, shift and fracture after environmental change.

Methods in motion

Walking audio, photo elicitation and trauma-aware approaches to lived experience.
